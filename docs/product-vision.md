Refine the existing STARTS Forward AI project. Do not rebuild the project from scratch. Keep the current design and functionality where appropriate, but improve the platform architecture, user experience, navigation, and AI functionality around the following vision.

## CORE PURPOSE

STARTS Forward is an AI-powered global health systems strengthening and collaboration platform that prepares students and early-career professionals to become the next generation of health leaders.

STARTS stands for:

**Science • Technology • Research • Action • Transforming Solutions**

The platform should connect:

**Students + Communities + Universities + NGOs + Researchers + Health Systems + Private Sector + Governments + Global Institutions**

The goal is to help users move from:

**Local Evidence → AI Analysis → Connection → Collaboration → Implementation → Policy/Institutional Influence → Measurable Impact**

Students and early-career professionals should be the primary users.

## THREE CORE PILLARS

### 1. Health Systems Strengthening

Help students understand health challenges across interconnected sectors, including:

* Public health
* Clinical healthcare
* Community health
* Health and wellness
* Government
* NGOs
* Private sector

The platform should encourage systems thinking rather than treating these fields as separate silos.

### 2. Innovation

Use AI to identify connections between:

* People
* Research
* Community evidence
* Countries
* Organizations
* Projects
* SDGs
* Health priorities
* Skills and expertise

For example, if student teams in Haiti, Rwanda, and Colombia are working on similar maternal-health problems, the AI should recognize the overlap and recommend collaboration.

### 3. Implementation

Help students transform research and community evidence into:

* Programs
* Interventions
* Policy recommendations
* Implementation plans
* Research outputs
* Dashboards
* Partnership proposals
* Program evaluations

The platform should emphasize turning evidence into action.

## AI AGENT SYSTEM

Refine the current AI architecture so the platform can eventually support these interconnected functions:

**Evidence Agent:** Organizes research, data, and community observations into usable evidence.

**Systems Agent:** Maps health problems across public health, clinical care, community health, wellness, policy, and private-sector systems.

**Match Agent:** Finds students, researchers, experts, NGOs, universities, and institutions working on related problems.

**SDG Agent:** Connects projects with relevant Sustainable Development Goals and global health priorities.

**Collaboration Agent:** Recommends cross-country and cross-sector collaborations.

**Implementation Agent:** Helps transform findings into actionable programs and interventions.

**Influence Agent:** Identifies which organizations, governments, agencies, experts, or decision-makers could act on the evidence.

**Opportunity Agent:** Connects users with relevant grants, fellowships, conferences, consultations, working groups, research opportunities, and projects.

**Impact Agent:** Tracks what happened after research or recommendations were produced.

These do not necessarily need to appear as separate chatbots. Design them as coordinated capabilities within one intelligent platform.

## BUILD AN “INFLUENCE LADDER”

Create a feature that helps users understand how evidence can move from a community to decision-makers.

Example:

**Community Evidence**
↓
**Local Health System / Organization**
↓
**NGO or Implementing Partner**
↓
**Government / Ministry of Health**
↓
**Regional Institution**
↓
**WHO / UN / Global Institution**
↓
**Relevant Policy or Intergovernmental Mechanism**

The AI should identify missing connections and recommend people, organizations, research, or partnerships that could help users move their work to the next level.

The core question should be:

**“What problem are you solving, what evidence do you have, who else is solving it, and who has the power to act on it?”**

Users should be able to see when multiple teams in different countries are addressing similar problems.

Position South Florida as the first STARTS Forward coordination hub and **gateway to the Americas**, particularly connecting:

**South Florida ↔ Caribbean ↔ Latin America ↔ Global Network**

Do not present STARTS as a South Florida organization simply trying to become international.

Present South Florida as the strategic starting hub of a distributed global network.

Use high-quality imagery/visual sections representing:

1. Miami/South Florida skyline and international connectivity
2. South Florida's connection with the Caribbean
3. South Florida's connection with Latin America

**STARTS South Florida Hub**
↓
**Caribbean Nodes**

**Latin American Nodes**

**African & Other Global Nodes**

**University + Faculty Champion + Student Team + Community/Institutional Partner**

## PAID EXPERIENCE / MICRO-PROJECT MODEL

Design the architecture so STARTS can eventually support paid institutional projects.

Example:

An NGO posts:

**“We need to understand barriers to maternal-health referrals in Haiti.”**

STARTS could recommend and assemble:

**Haitian students + South Florida students + Faculty Mentor + NGO Technical Lead**

The institution provides the problem, mentorship, resources, and funding.

Students provide research, analysis, community insight, and implementation support.

STARTS provides AI infrastructure, training, matching, methodology, collaboration tools, and quality assurance.

Students should eventually be able to receive compensation for meaningful project work.

Avoid designing a system where students simply become unpaid labor for institutions.

## PRODUCT PRINCIPLE

STARTS Forward should NOT feel like:

* LinkedIn
* An internship board
* A generic AI chatbot
* A social network
* An online course platform

It should feel like an:

**AI-powered global health intelligence, collaboration, workforce development, and implementation ecosystem.**

The platform should help users answer:

**What health problem am I working on?**

**What evidence exists?**

**Who else is working on it?**

**Where in the world are they working?**

**What can we learn from each other?**

**Who has the expertise we need?**

**Who has the authority to act?**

**How do we turn this evidence into implementation?**

**What measurable impact resulted?**

## DESIGN DIRECTION

Maintain a sophisticated, modern, global-health-focused interface.

Prioritize:

* Visual clarity
* Interactive maps
* Network visualization
* Project cards
* SDG indicators
* Evidence dashboards
* Collaboration recommendations
* Influence pathways
* AI-generated insights
* Impact tracking

The experience should feel credible enough for universities, NGOs, governments, and global institutions while remaining intuitive and exciting for students and early-career professionals.

Review the existing codebase first. Preserve strong existing components and functionality. Refactor or expand only where necessary to align the current product with this architecture and vision.
